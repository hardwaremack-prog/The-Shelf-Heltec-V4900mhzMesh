The Shelf, on a Heltec V4, V3 or ESP32

The full Shelf web app for your home, served from a little Heltec LoRa board and a microSD card. Photos, music, videos, games, books, Notes, Docs, Sheets, Slides, Draw, Tidy Suite and Messages. It even chats with the other Shelves in your house.

Want The Shelf on a Windows, Mac, Linux or Raspberry Pi computer instead? Install it on a computer with one line.

1. Install it

Plug the board into this computer with a USB data cable, pick your board, then choose its port when Chrome asks. This works in Chrome or Edge on a PC, Mac or Raspberry Pi. It's a 5 MB install, so give it a minute or two.

Coming from v1.8 or earlier? Install v1.9 once over USB here, and leave "erase" unticked. It gives the board a second firmware slot, so from then on it updates itself over Wi-Fi: open its settings page and choose Update firmware. This one-time change clears files kept on the board's own flash (boards without a card); files on a microSD card, your Wi-Fi, admin password and settings all stay. If your files are on the board itself, back it up first (below).
This replaces whatever is on the board, Meshtastic included. You can put Meshtastic back any time from flasher.meshtastic.org.

2. Wire a microSD card

Use the small 3.3 V SPI module whose pins read 3v3 · CS · MOSI · CLK · MISO · GND. It has no regulator: 3V3 only, never 5V. The card goes on four neighbouring J3 pins, the same on the V4 and the V3. With no card fitted, The Shelf uses about 9 MB of a V4's own flash, or about 2.5 MB of a V3's.

Or plug it straight in. 42 · 41 · 40 · 39 are in the same order as the module's CS · MOSI · CLK · MISO. Bend up its 3v3 and GND pins so they skip GPIO 45 and 38, then add two short jumpers to GND and 3V3 at the start of the row.

Using Heltec's GPS kit on a V4? The in-line spot shares GPIO 39–42 with the GNSS connector, so wire the card with six short wires instead: 3v3 → 3V3, GND → GND, CS → GPIO 4, MISO → GPIO 6 (J3 row), CLK → GPIO 47, MOSI → GPIO 48 (J2 row). The Shelf 1.18 and later finds the card on either layout, and the V4 gets a GPS tab with a map, altitude, speed, heading, satellites and your grid square.

The same, seen from underneath.

Without the GPS kit, the in-line spot above is simplest. V4-R8: GPIO 40 powers its screen, so it keeps the older wiring: CS 4, MISO 6, CLK 47, MOSI 48 (R8 diagram). Coming from v1.5 or earlier on a V4 or V3? Move the four signal wires to the new pins. Use a FAT32 card; 32 GB cards come that way.

Plain ESP32 (NodeMCU / DevKitC): it must have a microSD card: the 4 MB of flash is all taken by the firmware, its update loader and the web app. A 128×64 SSD1306 screen is optional; with one, it shows the same screen pages as the Heltec boards.

Recommended: in line on the bottom side, one jumper. The module plugs straight onto five header pins in a row on the underside, GND · 19 · 18 · 5 · 17, its GND end towards the antenna. Bend its 3v3 pin away so it misses GPIO 16, and run one jumper from it to 3V3 on the other row. Firmware 1.10.1 and later finds the card on this wiring by itself.

Or with six wires on the ESP32's usual SPI pins: 3v3 → 3V3, GND → GND, CS → GPIO 5, MOSI → GPIO 23, CLK → GPIO 18, MISO → GPIO 19. Wired diagram · board flipped over.

Adding a screen: a 0.96" I2C OLED plugs straight onto 27 · 14 · 12 · GND with no wires (in-line screen diagram), or takes four wires to GND, 3V3, GPIO 22 (SCL) and GPIO 21 (SDA) (wired screen diagram).

Copying or backing up a card on Windows: SD Card Imager saves a whole card to one compressed image file and writes it back onto another card (Windows 10/11, nothing to install: unzip and double-click SD Card Imager.cmd).

3. Connect it to your Wi-Fi

  1. Power the board. The screen shows Join shelf-v4-xxxx (every board's own Wi-Fi has the board's name). (A plain ESP32 skips setup: it makes its own Wi-Fi shelf-esp32-xxxx, password theshelf. Join it and open http://10.1.1.250. Joining your home Wi-Fi is optional, from its settings page.)
  2. Join that Wi-Fi on your phone. The password is theshelf. The setup page opens; if it doesn't, go to http://10.1.1.250.
  3. Tap Find networks, pick your home Wi-Fi and type its password. The board restarts.
  4. Back on your home Wi-Fi, open the address on the board's screen, or http://shelf-v4-xxxx.local (shelf-v3-xxxx.local on a V3, shelf-esp32-xxxx.local on a plain ESP32).

Make room: insane compression for your music and photos

Upload music and photos through the board's page and they're shrunk in your browser on the way in: songs become Opus, which sounds about the same as an MP3 at about a third of the size and keeps its title, artist and album art. Photos become AVIF, and iPhone HEIC photos work too. The board stores only the small copy, so your microSD card holds about 3 times the music and over 10 times the photos, and everything streams faster over Wi-Fi. (Untick Shrink music and photos first in the upload box to send files as they are.)

Take it anywhere

On the board's settings page, choose Use its own Wi-Fi and it makes its own network, so The Shelf works in the car, at camp, or anywhere without Wi-Fi. Join it and open http://10.1.1.250. Your phone shows "No Internet Connection" and keeps using cellular data for texts and apps while it stays connected to The Shelf. The screen shows the network name, the password, the IP, and a battery meter, so you can run it from a LiPo.

Meshed Comms

A Meshed Comms tab lets your boards text each other over the V4's LoRa radio, with no Wi-Fi or internet needed. Switch it on and you see the message log and can send to everyone. V3 and V4 boards share the same mesh. Every board with Coms on also relays for the others, with no hop limit, and a board that joins late is caught up on the last 12 hours by its neighbors. Fit the LoRa antenna before switching it on.

2.4 GHz too. Every board also sends and hears Coms over 2.4 GHz (ESP-NOW, the ESP32's own board-to-board frames), so plain ESP32 boards join the mesh through their Meshed Comms tab, and Heltec boards carry messages between the 2.4 GHz and 900 MHz boards. No router in between, and phones can stay joined to a board's own Wi-Fi. Boards hear each other over 2.4 GHz only on the same Wi-Fi channel: channel 1 on their own Wi-Fi, the router's channel on a home Wi-Fi. When a board meets one it hasn't heard for 20 minutes, it asks for the last 12 hours again, so messages travel with people carrying boards around.

Manage boards over 900MHz

The Coms tab lists every board on the mesh. Click one, type its admin password, and connect. You can check its battery, Wi-Fi and storage, turn read-only on or off, wake or sleep its screen, set its power, list its files, or restart it, all over the radio. Commands are signed with the board's password (never sent) rather than encrypted, as amateur rules require.

Read-only mode

Set an admin password on the board's settings page and switch on read-only: everyone can browse, play and chat, but only someone with the password can change things. A switch in the sidebar unlocks write mode for 8 hours. Forgot the password? Hold PRG for 12 seconds.

The board's screen

The address to open is always on the bottom line, and the battery meter is in the top corner. The screen sleeps after 5 minutes; press PRG to wake it. New Coms messages show for 15 seconds.

Updates over Wi-Fi

From v1.9, open the board's settings page and choose Update firmware (or go to /update on the board). Check for update downloads the newest version from this site and installs it; your files and settings stay. On the board's own Wi-Fi, where there's no internet, download your board's file first (V4 · V4-R8 · V4 MMC · V3 · ESP32) and use Update from a file. The board's screen shows the progress. The update page is built into the firmware, so it still works if an update is ever cut off.

Back up or restore the whole board

Save everything on the board's own flash to one compressed file: the firmware, your Wi-Fi, admin password, Coms history, and any files kept on the board when no card is fitted. Restore puts it all back exactly as it was, on this board or a replacement of the same kind. Plug in over USB-C like an install. The microSD card is backed up separately, over Wi-Fi, from the board's settings page.

Back up

Reads the whole flash (16 MB on a V4, 8 MB on a V3) and saves a .bin.gz. About 3–5 minutes.

Restore

Pick a backup file. It replaces everything on the board's flash. The card isn't touched.

Serial console

See what the board prints over USB: start-up, which card mode it found, and the details if it ever crashes. Plug the board in with a USB data cable, click Connect and pick its port (Chrome or Edge). When the board restarts its USB drops for a moment; the console reconnects by itself. If it crashed, Copy log and send it over: the lines in red say where.
